## 1. No result exists. Nothing here is an empirical finding.

The most important limitation is the simplest: **no simulation has been run, no fit performed, no
coverage computed.** `data/processed/` contains one ingested reference table and nothing else. The
fit refuses to run without sourced θ — `chipsim/transport/fit.py` calls `_require_sourced_theta` (cited by symbol: a line number drifts, a symbol does not)
— and θ does not exist.

This is by design, not by omission: every scientific input is human-owned under Global Constraint 1,
and the genre was chosen *because* of that. But it bounds every claim in this report to methodology.

**Explicitly not claimed:** no results observed; no fit performed; the 3-fold ordering gate untested;
coverage uncomputed; no wet-lab validation; the ODE is a proof-of-concept two-compartment model, not
a physiological one.

**Human-owned Stage 2 inputs still absent**, measured against the tree: θ priors, the transport

## 2. The content guard's scope is narrower than its headline, and the headline is qualified by ruling

CTO #512 item 1 makes this binding. The guard classifies **141 of 141 in-scope sites** (at commit `af17cd2`), and that
sentence **may not appear unqualified anywhere**. Qualified, it reads:

> classified for **provenance adjacency** by a predicate later shown to be satisfiable by its own
> explanation.

Three limits, each established by measurement rather than assertion:

1. **W and F test provenance adjacency only.** The standing content rule has three prohibitions — no
   real accessions, no database-coined titles, **no name-to-identifier associations**. The classifier
   tests the first indirectly and **measures neither of the last two**.
2. **The accession detector is shaped for one database.** Another database's accession form is
   structurally invisible to it. The production module already disclaims the name half entirely:
   names are an unbounded vocabulary and there is no detector for them.
3. **The citation predicate is positively correlated with the violation it exists to leave visible.**
   A block naming a compound beside its source identifier, its structure and a retrieval date is
   simultaneously the strongest citation match *and* a textbook name-to-identifier association. A
   site can be excluded from scrutiny *because* it sits beside the thing the rule forbids.

## 5. The recurring defect family, and that it was never closed

Across nine gates one family recurred: **the claim lives in prose — a test name, a docstring, an
assertion message, a reason string, a route column, a typed count — the check lives in code, and
nothing fails when they diverge.**

Gate 9 found it in the mechanisms built to stop it. Five readers converged; the sharpest instance is
that **the citation predicate is satisfied by the worked example inside its own docstring, and by the
positive-control fixture of the test written to prove that cannot happen** — subtracting 53 of 142
sites at the time of measurement, four of them the test file's own.

Also unrepaired and recorded by measurement: the scanner numbers lines by counting line feeds while
the classifier splits on nine further code points (latent, 0 live instances); the sum-identity test
asserts a different identity from the clause; the reporting harness's union rule is documented and
then discarded (the loop body is `pass`, D-05 — still live; the *hardcoded host path* named here in an
earlier draft was already fixed, and the register lists it under "already fixed", so two Stage 1
documents disagreed about the same defect until 2026-09-30); the anti-vacuity guard pins a syntactic class that omits the
property making it anti-vacuous — with that one pattern removed, the original defect can be restored
and the suite stays green.

**An observed instance in committed evidence:** gate 7's committed artifact carries a commit subject
belonging to another workstream, because the instrument that enforces the content constraint wrote
unguarded free text into the surface it protects. It was **not** edited — retroactive edits to
committed evidence are the principal's — and r2.50 mechanism (5) prevents recurrence.

**One instance in this report's own production:** a snapshot size published in the gate-9 verdict was
measured at the wrong point in time (creation rather than post-review), making a stated comparison
like-for-unlike. Self-reported, corrected inline in the findings file, and recorded here because
*"counts are regenerated, never typed"* does not by itself catch a number measured at the wrong
moment.

### A gate rule was relaxed after it blocked this gate (2026-09-30)

Recorded because the sequence, not the rule, is what a referee should weigh.

The artifact scan's verdict was ruled (CTO #540 (1b)) to fail on a file reading as cited **outside**
the classified roots, "where nothing else reports it". Applied faithfully, that left exactly one fatal
condition standing: three **tracked dispatch records** that satisfy the citation predicate. They are
committed audit evidence, and retroactive edits to committed evidence are the principal's — the same
rule under which a gate-7 artifact carrying another workstream's text was deliberately left unedited.
So the gate could not pass and could not be made to pass by the author.

The principal amended the rule: those files are **reported**, not fatal. The reasoning is sound — no
classifier covers dispatch records, so a cited dispatch swallows no site into bucket F, and the harm
the fatality existed to prevent cannot occur there; the scan now names each file by path. The verdict
went from FAIL to PASS on that amendment alone.

**What is recorded is the order of events.** The rule was relaxed *after* it blocked, on evidence
surfaced by the author, who had an interest in the gate passing — and the author had, one ruling
earlier, declined to choose between two readings for precisely that reason. Both the amendment and its
timing are in the instrument's own record (`verdict_note`), in the approval trail, and here. A referee
weighing this report's integrity claims should know that one of its gates was widened at the last
boundary, and should be able to find that fact without reading a diff.

The alternative was not a quiet fix. It was closing Stage 1 at the scope reached, which row 61 already
contemplates as a result. That the alternative was live is part of the record too.

### The mechanism itself, enumerated — the Stage 1 result under row 61

Row 61 (principal, verbatim *"Stop; the mechanism is the result"*) closes repair and makes the
following the finding. One mechanism recurs, and it is now better evidenced than any single defect in
this report:

> **A fix is verified against the thing that was changed, not against the property the claim names.**

Six instances, dated, in the order they were introduced, each naming the repair that introduced it.
Four of the six were introduced *by the repair of a previous instance*, which is the part that makes
this a mechanism rather than a list of mistakes.

1. **(2026-09-29, introduced by the creation of `redact-records.py`.)** The audit-surface path guard
   got a test named for it — `…REFUSES_a_path_outside_the_audit_surface` — which points outside the
   *repository* and is caught by an earlier branch. The guard itself was executed by nothing. What was
   verified: that a refusal happened. What the claim names: *which* refusal. Deleting the guard
   entirely left the suite green. **Repaired 2026-09-30 (C-2).**
2. **(2026-09-29, introduced by the repair of blocker 4.)** `raises=AssertionError` was added so a
   strict xfail could not accept any exception. What was verified: that the marker carried `raises=`.
   What the claim names: that only the *registered divergence* is accepted. A bare `assert` anywhere in
   the call satisfied `AssertionError`, so an unrelated failure retired the registered gate-6 finding
   with the file byte-identical. **Repaired 2026-09-30 (C-3), by giving the divergence a type.**
3. **(2026-09-29, introduced by the repair of the round-1 scope finding.)** Fix (vii) added tests that
   the bracket's metadata channels carry digests. What was verified: that `differences()` still
   reported a change. What the claim names: that each entry *is the digest of its own line*. The
   assertion's second disjunct ("some entry is 64 hex") is true by construction, so digesting a
   constant — destroying entry identity — leaves both tests passing. **Deferred (R-05).**
4. **(2026-09-29, introduced by the repair of the round-1 H4 finding.)** The scan's globs were widened
   from 10 files to 622 and H4 restated. What was verified: that the glob reached more files. What the
   claim names: that the measured surface covers *the classified scope*. No glob reached
   `projects/lung-on-chipsim/` at all, so "0 cited inside the classified scope" was computed over a
   partial intersection and published as a property of the scope. The qualifier presented as an honest
   weakening is what restored the original strength. **Repaired 2026-09-30 (C-1).**
5. **(2026-09-30, introduced by the repair of instance 4.)** H4's citation was moved off a superseded
   record. What was verified: that H4 now cites the current record. What the claim names: that claims
   cite current records. **H1, one row above, still cited the superseded 10-file record** — whose file
   list omits the documents asserting H1. Seven of nine record citations were stale.
   **Repaired 2026-09-30 (H-7), and the class closed by `claims-vs-records.py`.**
6. **(2026-09-30, introduced by the repair of instance 4, same day, caught before commit.)** The first
   attempt at C-1 widened the surface with filesystem globs and swept **26,623 files** — the
   virtualenv and thousands of untracked per-run journal copies. What was verified: that the globs now
   reached the classified roots. What the claim names: that the surface *equals* the scope — and a
   filesystem glob and `git ls-files` are two enumerations of one set, which is the defect being
   repaired. **Repaired the same day, by using one corpus.**

**What this is and is not evidence of.** It is evidence that the family is open, that it survives
repair, and that repair is one of its vectors. It is **not** evidence that the mechanisms of r2.50
work: four of the six instances were introduced by those very repairs, and every one of the six was
found by a *reader* — a reviewer or the author re-reading — never by an instrument. The one
instrument-level closure achieved here (`surface_vs_scope`, and `claims-vs-records.py`) closes two of
the six classes and is itself untested against the other four.

Two further observations belong to the same mechanism and are recorded rather than repaired: a
**fabricated finding** was nearly published in this round — a "48-vs-46 instrument disagreement" that
does not exist, being a fresh measurement compared against a stale record — and the paragraph in this
section *about* counts that drift carried three mutually inconsistent counts of its own.

**A second instance in this report's own production (2026-09-29):** the approval-log citations in this
report used **two incompatible numbering schemes in one sentence** — physical row position for the
time-box and descope rows, the `#` column for the gate-9 verdict — so [G3], the sharpest integrity
claim, resolved under neither reading alone. Found by a reader, not by a check: no instrument verifies
that a "row N" in prose names the row it means. Repaired by keying numbered rows `#N` and
non-numbered rows by timestamp, stated once in methods §7. The **log's own route text** at `#54` and
`#58` carries the same defect (both say "row 54" meaning the time box); the log is append-only and
hash-locked, so that text is left uncorrected and recorded here instead. This is the family exactly:
the claim is a citation in prose, and nothing fails when it points somewhere else.

**A third instance, about the measurement scope itself (2026-09-29, restated 2026-09-30):** the
content scan's globs were non-recursive and its record reports **10 files scanned**, while the
claim it supported spoke of "every artifact". The verdict was not wrong about what it measured; it was
wrong about what a reader would take it to cover, and nothing in the output distinguished the two. The
surface now reports **724 files scanned**.

**This paragraph previously carried three mutually inconsistent figures of its own** — "14 of 539
files — 2.6%" and "widened to 621" — in the section whose subject is counts that drift. Measured: the
superseded record's own `files_scanned` is **10** (14 appears only in a source comment), the
widened figure is **724**, and **539 appears in no artifact anywhere**. The percentage is removed
rather than recomputed: 10 and 724 were measured over different trees at different commits,
so any ratio of them is like-for-unlike, and inventing a fourth number here would repeat the defect a
third time. Each count is named against the record that holds it.

The direction is what matters: a gate that measures a small fraction of the surface it names produces
output identical to a gate that measured all of it.

**A fourth instance, about latency rather than detection (2026-09-29):** the mechanical redaction
replaced **58 accession-shaped and 16 structure-shaped tokens** across 9 audit records. The count 58
equals the count named in an urgent incident dispatch dated **2026-09-25** — that escalation was
about these records, and the values were still present four days later. Detection is recorded as
having worked; **closure** is recorded as having taken four days, and no instrument measured the gap
between the two.

## 7. What a referee should take from this section

This report's subject is a methodology, and its central artifact — the approval log — is a
contemporaneous, hash-locked record of revisions, corrections and reversals. The limitations above
are not incidental to that claim; **they are the evidence for it.** A methodology that could not
record its own failures would have produced a shorter section, and a less trustworthy one.
