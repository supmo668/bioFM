## 0 · REPORT EARLY — the P-gp asymmetry repeats, and the mechanism is the same rule

The CTO asked to be told early if the P-gp asymmetry repeated on this axis. **It repeats, almost
exactly.**

| axis | yes | no | unknown | of 26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-glycoprotein (ratified, §1) | 3 | **0** | 23 | 26 |
| OCT1 after pass 2 (exploratory) | 3 | **0** | 23 | 26 |

The coincidence of the totals is arithmetic accident; **the reason the negative column is empty is
not.** On both axes the closest candidate negative fails on the *same* rule — **substrate ≠
inhibitor**:

- On the P-gp axis, Mukherjee 2012 was disqualified because it measured inhibition of a probe.
- On this axis, the single best candidate negative — **tiotropium** — comes from an experiment that
  measures *failure to inhibit* MPP⁺ uptake. Failure to inhibit is weaker evidence than inhibition,
  and inhibition was already too weak to carry a verdict. It also pools OCT1 with OCT3 (see §3).

**This is a methodological finding, not a coincidence.** The transporter literature publishes
positive transport determinations and very rarely publishes a per-compound negative determination.
An evidential bar that requires a *named transporter, named species, direct transport of the
compound itself* is therefore structurally unable to populate a negative group from the published
record — on any transporter, not only P-gp. **That is §1's claim about method rather than biology,
now with a second and independent axis behind it.** §1 asserts it for one transporter; this pass
suggests the cause is the shape of the literature.

I am not drafting that as a paper claim. It is a finding for the principal to rule on, and it would
need a third axis before it could be stated as a general result.

## 2 · The two NEW verdicts' evidence, quoted from the FULL TEXT

**Salbutamol — `yes`. Pirbuterol — `yes`.** Römer, Lazzarin, Neumann, Lindemann, Meyer-Tönnies,
Stockner & Tzvetkov, *Journal of Biological Chemistry* 2025;301(4):108318. DOI
`10.1016/j.jbc.2025.108318` (PMC12005293). **Full text retrieved and read at source** via PubMed
Central; according to PubMed.

Verbatim, from the Results:

> "All tested betaagonists were good OCT1 substrates (). OCT1 wildtype showed increased uptake
> ranging from 4.6-fold (salbutamol) to 77-fold (pirbuterol) compared with empty vector control
> cells (pcDNA5)."

The basis is **direct uptake of each compound itself**, not inhibition of a probe — Experimental
procedures:

> "Intracellular substrate concentrations were subsequently measured and normalized to the total
> protein content in the samples"

by LC–MS/MS, in cells described as:

> "All cell lines used in this work were either T-REx-293 cells (Life Technologies) or therefrom
> originating stably overexpressing cell lines"

and the negative control is explicit:

> "T-Rex-293 cells do not exhibit a relevant background of endogenous OCT1 expression"

**Both rules the P-gp axis applies are satisfied:** the transporter is named (human OCT1, hOCT1,
distinguished in the same paper from murine Oct1), and the readout is transport of the compound,
against an empty-vector control.

**One caveat I state rather than leave implied.** The paper's subject is the **YER motif mutants**,
not a substrate survey. Salbutamol and pirbuterol appear as wild-type reference measurements inside
a mutagenesis study. The wild-type determination is nonetheless direct and quantified against
vector control, so it carries — but a reader must not cite this as a substrate-screening paper.
This is the same caveat pass 1 recorded for Meyer 2022, where human OCT1 was the comparator and the
dog ortholog was the subject.

## 3 · Tiotropium — the candidate negative, and why it does NOT qualify

This is the closest the published record came to a negative on this axis, and recording *why it
fails* is more useful than the row it produced.

Barilli, Visigalli, Ferrari, Di Lascia, Riccardi, Puccini, Dall'Asta & Rotoli, *Biomedicines*
2020;8(5):127. DOI `10.3390/biomedicines8050127` (PMC7277691). **Full text read at source**;
according to PubMed. Verbatim:

> "ipratropium significantly reduced the basolateral uptake of MPPin both EpiAirway™ and Calu-3
> monolayers, employed for comparison, while the two other drugs were ineffective in both cell
> models. This finding clearly points to an involvement of OCT1 and OCT3 transporters in the
> interaction with ipratropium, but not with tiotropium, nor glycopyrrolate."

**Three independent reasons this cannot be a `no`:**

1. **It is an inhibition readout, and a null one.** The measurement is tiotropium's effect on MPP⁺
   uptake. Inhibition is already disqualified as substrate evidence by this pass's own rules;
   *absence* of inhibition is one step weaker again. A low-affinity substrate need not inhibit a
   probe at the concentration tested.
2. **It does not isolate OCT1.** The quoted sentence names "OCT1 and OCT3" together. The paper
   separates the two pharmacologically for MPP⁺ (quinidine vs corticosterone) but the bronchodilator
   experiment reports total basolateral uptake. An OCT1+OCT3 result is not an OCT1 result — the same
   rule that made an OCT2/MATE result unusable in pass 1.
3. **The authors' own word is "interaction", not "transport".** Reading "does not interact" as "is
   not a substrate" would be a correct reading of a real result converted into a claim the result
   does not make. That is this project's recurring defect and I am declining it here.

## 5 · A CONFLICT in the 2025 literature on salbutamol, recorded not resolved

Two 2025 papers sharing a senior author state apparently opposite things.

**Rönnpagel, Morof, Römer, Meyer-Tönnies & Tzvetkov,** *Drug Metab Dispos* 2025;53(5):100074, DOI
`10.1016/j.dmd.2025.100074` (PMC12163485), full text read. From §3.4:

> "Although fenoterol was transported only by OCT1, the structurally related salbutamol was
> transported only by OCT3."

Read alone, that is an OCT1 **negative** for salbutamol. **It must not be read alone**, because the
same paper elsewhere measures OCT1-mediated salbutamol uptake:

> "The uptake of metformin was 5.1-fold and of salbutamol was 4.5-fold stronger in mouse than in
> human OCT1."

— a statement that presupposes human-OCT1 salbutamol uptake exists; and §3.3 reports IC₅₀ values for
inhibition *of salbutamol uptake* in human-OCT1 cells. Salbutamol is also one of the eight cocktail
substrates the paper selected as "strongly transported by OCT1 in vitro".

**The most likely reconciliation** — which I am proposing, not asserting — is that salbutamol is a
**weak** human-OCT1 substrate, so a paralog comparison can read as "only OCT3" while direct
measurement still detects uptake. Römer 2025's 4.6-fold is the **lowest** of all beta-agonists it
tested, which fits. If so, §3.4's sentence is an overstatement of a selectivity comparison, not an
OCT1 negative.

**Consequence for the verdict.** I drafted `yes` for salbutamol on Römer 2025's direct
vector-controlled measurement, and I am flagging rather than burying the conflicting sentence. **If
the principal prefers, salbutamol is the one row in this pass that could defensibly be returned to
`unknown` pending table-level resolution.** It should not become a `no` on §3.4's sentence alone.

## 6 · An unexpected bearing on ledger §1 — OCT1's airway localisation is disputed, like P-gp's

§1 carries, as a cited limitation, that P-gp is largely absent from human airway epithelium and that
in the one model where it is functionally present it sits on the opposite membrane. Barilli 2020
reports the **same shape of problem for OCT1**, which is worth the principal's attention because it
strengthens §1's framing rather than threatening it. Verbatim:

> "EpiAirway™ display an OCT transport activity which is confined to the basolateral compartment."

> "This finding is in line with immunofluorescent studies performed in human bronchi showing OCT3 in
> the entire plasma membrane of basal cells and on the basolateral membrane of intermediate cells.
> In the same model, however, OCT1 was observed in the luminal membrane of ciliated epithelial
> cells."

So OCT1's membrane localisation in human airway is **disputed between a functional assay and an
immunofluorescence study of human bronchi** — basolateral versus luminal. An apical-dosed inhaled
drug meets a basolateral transporter only after crossing the epithelium.

**And a possible fifth source for §1's P-gp claim, which I am NOT adding to any count.** The same
paper states:

> "In our studies, however, the contribution of P-gp has been recently excluded in EpiAirway™"

That is a **citation to the authors' own prior work, not a finding of this paper.** §1 says "four
sources concur"; this does not make it five. It is a lead to a possible fifth primary source, and it
would have to be read at its own source before any count changed. **§1's count stays at four.**
