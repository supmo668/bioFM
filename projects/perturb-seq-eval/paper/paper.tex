\begin{abstract}
A multi-agent group-generation system, as operated here, spends test-time
compute uniformly across tasks. That a single agent's own vote distribution
can gate its test-time compute is already reported by
\textsc{CATTS}~\citep{lee2026catts}; we ask the multi-agent question: whether
the distribution of stated confidences and critiques across a group of
role-specialised agents predicts how hard a task is, which would let the
group's compute be allocated adaptively. Taking as testbed a five-agent orchestrator for Perturb-seq
response prediction inspired by \textsc{CellForge}~\citep{chen2025cellforge}
and operated via \textsc{MassGen}~\citep{massgen2025github}, we define four
trace metrics --- Agent Confidence Entropy (ACE), Critique
Severity Dispersion (CSD), a convergence signal ($\Delta C$), and a Winner
Flip Rate (WFR) --- and their composite, the Task Difficulty Index (TDI).
We pre-register five hypotheses with criteria: attainable error on
\textsc{Adamson 2016}~\citep{adamson2016perturbseq} (CRISPR interference) and
\textsc{Norman 2019}~\citep{norman2019perturbseq} (CRISPR activation), the
Architect agent's backbone-choice entropy, the rank correlation of each TDI
component with held-out error, and cross-dataset transfer of a calibrated
TDI. The design is $41$ held-out K562 tasks ($21$ Adamson, $15$ Norman
singletons, $5$ Norman doublets) drawn deterministically, a trainer sweep of
\resNTrainerRecordsPerTask{} records per task and three fixed-length agentic
lifecycle runs per task, all in one process. The agents are large language models (LLMs) from a pinned
roster of Anthropic Claude models (Haiku 4.5 for the four proposer roles,
Sonnet 5.5 for the Validator) whose served model is recorded and asserted
per call; a run with any rule-based fallback step is invalid. Every run records its git revision, resolved
configuration, library versions, dataset SHA-256 digests, task plan and
label contract. We find that \resGateSummary. The trainer sweep reaches
the pre-registered error on both screens, and the Architect's choice of backbone
varies above the pre-registered entropy floor. Within each screen, the drop in the
agents' confidence across rounds, and the composite that inherits it, rank tasks by
held-out error. From the second round on, however, every agent's prompt carries the
previous round's held-out error, so that drop is stated after an error has been
shown. In an exploratory analysis, the mean confidence stated before any feedback does
not rank tasks by error ($\rho=\explAdaConfRZeroMsdRho$ on Adamson and
$\explNorConfRZeroMsdRho$ on Norman, both intervals including zero). Read with the
design and that analysis, the pre-registered pass is consistent with stated confidence
falling further where a larger error was shown; it is not evidence that the group's
confidence anticipates difficulty. The composite fitted
on Adamson did not meet the pre-registered transfer criterion on Norman
($\rho=\resHFiveRho$ against a criterion of $>\resHFiveThreshold$). The point
estimates for confidence entropy, an evenness index over the agents, run against the
pre-registered direction in both screens; this is exploratory, and no claim is made.
The preflight probe and recommender are specified but not evaluated.
The values reported in the earlier release of this work are superseded in
full; the defects behind each are listed in the appendix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Test-time compute (TTC) has emerged as a first-class axis of capability in
large language models (LLMs). \citet{snell2024scaling} showed that an
\emph{optimally allocated} per-prompt TTC budget lets a small model match a
$14\times$ larger model under FLOPs-matched comparison, with the optimal
allocation depending on the difficulty of the prompt. The recent taxonomy of
\citet{zhang2025ttssurvey} organises the exploding TTC literature along four
orthogonal axes --- \emph{what} to scale, \emph{how} to scale, \emph{where} to
scale, and \emph{how well} the method scales --- but the prominent
applications to date address \emph{single-chain} reasoning: parallel samples
scored by a process reward model (PRM)~\citep{lightman2024prm}, sequential
self-revision, tree search~\citep{yao2023tot}, or internal chain-of-thought
trained via reinforcement learning~\citep{deepseek2025r1,muennighoff2025s1}.

The multi-agent case is structurally different but no less TTC-laden. A
group-generation system such as \textsc{CellForge}~\citep{chen2025cellforge}
dispatches several specialised agents --- typically a data curator, a
literature/prior agent, a model architect, a trainer, and a biological
validator --- and coordinates them through propose-critique-vote rounds. As
operated here, such a system spends compute \emph{uniformly}: the same number of agents
for the same number of rounds regardless of whether the task is a
well-characterised single-gene perturbation or a novel combinatorial one.
\emph{This paper asks whether the group's own internal dynamics carry enough
signal to allocate its compute adaptively}, and tests that question under a
pre-registered design so that either answer is informative. The answer on this
run is narrower than the question: the trace feature that meets its criterion is
stated after the agents have been shown a held-out error, and, in an exploratory
analysis added after the outcome, the mean confidence stated before any such
feedback does not rank tasks (\S\ref{subsec:exploratory}). No pre-registered or
claimed signal here is available before compute is spent.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make four contributions, all within the
empirical frame of Perturb-seq response prediction~\citep{norman2019perturbseq,%
adamson2016perturbseq} but generalising to any propose-critique-vote system:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We test whether a \emph{multi-agent group's} confidence/critique
        distribution is an explicit item-difficulty signal. We formalise four
        observables of such a run, each computed from the trace --- ACE, CSD,
        $\Delta C$/$\Delta$ACE, and WFR --- over the stated confidences and
        critiques of $N$ role-specialised agents, within and across rounds,
        and combine them into a scalar Task Difficulty Index (TDI). That an
        agent's own vote distribution can gate test-time compute is reported
        by \textsc{CATTS}~\citep{lee2026catts}, whose statistics are computed
        over $N$ candidate actions sampled from one base model at a single
        step (their \S3.1) under the stated assumption of ``a fixed base
        agent'' (their \S2); we claim nothing about that case.
  \item We specify a \emph{preflight probe}: a single shallow round whose
        four-dimensional signature is the feature vector of a recommender
        over orchestration hyperparameters
        $\varphi = (N, R, \textrm{backbone})$. The probe and the recommender
        are specified and released; neither is fitted or evaluated in this
        paper (\S\ref{sec:probe}, \S\ref{sec:limits}).
  \item We pre-register five hypotheses and their criteria
        (\S\ref{sec:results}) and test them
        on $41$ held-out K562 tasks from \textsc{Adamson 2016} and
        \textsc{Norman 2019}, with a trainer sweep and an agentic lifecycle
        run over the identical task set in one process.
  \item We release a provenance-complete run record --- git revision,
        resolved configuration, resolved library versions, dataset digests,
        task plan, label contract, and the serving model of every agent step
        --- together with the calibration harness and the
        \textsc{MassGen}-installable evaluation skill
        (\S\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} Both datasets are K562 Perturb-seq screens from the
scPerturb~\citep{peidli2024scperturb} repackaging (Zenodo record
$13350497$). Genome-scale validation on
\textsc{Replogle 2022}~\citep{replogle2022perturbseq} is the natural next step
and is enumerated in \S\ref{sec:limits}; the paper does not extrapolate
beyond K562.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Test-time compute scaling.} The modern TTC literature is usefully
organised by whether the extra compute is spent \emph{in parallel} (best-of-$N$
with a verifier~\citep{cobbe2021verifiers,lightman2024prm}, self-consistency
voting~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}), \emph{sequentially} (iterative
revision, self-refine), \emph{hybrid} (tree search, Monte Carlo tree search,
\citep{yao2023tot}), or \emph{internal} to the model
(\citep{deepseek2025r1,muennighoff2025s1}); see \citet{zhang2025ttssurvey} for
a taxonomy and \citet{wu2024smalltllm,liu2025rethinkingtts} for scaling-law
analyses. \citet{snell2024scaling} is the primary reference for the
difficulty-conditional compute-optimal idea that motivates our question in
the group setting.

\paragraph{Multi-agent TTC.} \textsc{AutoGen}~\citep{wu2023autogen} and
\textsc{MassGen}~\citep{massgen2025github} generalise propose-critique-vote to
heterogeneous agent pools; \textsc{Archon}~\citep{saad2024archon} and
\textsc{MAV}~\citep{lifshitz2025mav} treat inference-time architecture
\emph{itself} as the search space. \textsc{CellForge}~\citep{chen2025cellforge}
applies this pattern specifically to single-cell experimental design. We do
not claim the signal is new. Recent work reports gating test-time
compute on confidence- or consensus-type signals from an agent's own vote
distribution or from a group of agents~\citep{lee2026catts,morandi2026sprt};
we have read the sections cited below and have not reproduced or
re-derived their methods; beyond the design differences stated below, we
make no claim about how our signal relates to theirs. \textsc{CATTS}~\citep{lee2026catts} computes its uncertainty statistics over
$N$ candidate actions sampled from one base model at a single step (their
\S3.1), under the stated assumption of ``a fixed base agent'' (their \S2);
the quantities tested here are computed over the stated confidences of the
five role-specialised agents of one run, within and across rounds
(\S\ref{sec:tdi-real}).
The Wald-SPRT governor of \citet{morandi2026sprt} consumes an LLM judge's
consensus score on the agents' positions after each round (their \S III),
with the agent count held fixed in its real-LLM evaluation (their
\S VII-C14), and its author separates that across-agent signal from
``within-agent confidence'' (their \S VII-C17); the quantities tested here
are the agents' own stated confidences, and we do not gate rounds on them.

\paragraph{Perturb-seq and single-cell foundation models.} Perturb-seq
couples CRISPR perturbations with single-cell RNA-sequencing readouts,
offering a testbed for agentic scientific reasoning because every held-out
perturbation has a measured response against which a prediction is
scored~\citep{norman2019perturbseq,adamson2016perturbseq,
replogle2022perturbseq}. The difficulty proxy used in this paper is that held-out
prediction error (\S\ref{subsec:eval}) and nothing else. Pretrained single-cell foundation models such as
\textsc{scGPT}~\citep{cui2024scgpt}, \textsc{Geneformer}%
~\citep{theodoris2023geneformer}, \textsc{scFoundation}~\citep{hao2024scfoundation},
and \textsc{scPRINT}~\citep{kalfon2025scprint} are related work; none of them
is among the backbones evaluated here (\S\ref{subsec:backbones}).

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:setup}

We consider an orchestrator with $N$ specialised agents $\{\mathcal{A}_i\}_{i=1}^N$
coordinated over $R$ rounds, indexed $r = 0, \dots, R-1$. At each round $r$, every agent produces a
structured proposal with scalar confidence $c_i(r) \in [0,1]$ and every other
agent emits a critique with severity $S_{ij}(r) \in [0,1]$ (row $i$ critic,
column $j$ target; self-critique is undefined). A winner index $w(r)$ is
assigned by the orchestrator (typically $\mathrm{argmax}\, c - \overline{S}$,
where $\overline{S}$ is the mean severity received). We let
$\mathbf{C}(r)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^N$ and $\mathbf{S}(r)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N\times(N-1)}$
denote the per-round tuples.

A task $t$ has a latent intrinsic difficulty $d_t\!\in\![0,1]$. The team's
downstream utility on the task is proxied by the prediction error
$u(t, \varphi)$ on the held-out perturbation (the mean squared deviation, MSD, on its top-$20$
differentially expressed genes, \S\ref{subsec:eval}), where
$\varphi = (N, R, b)$ with $b$ a backbone choice.
\emph{Agentic hyperparameter tuning} is the problem of picking
$\varphi^\star\!\in\!\Phi$ minimising compute subject to a target error ---
the multi-agent analogue of \citet{snell2024scaling}'s compute-optimal
single-chain allocation.

\section{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Agent Confidence Entropy (ACE)}

Let $p(r) = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{C}(r)/\tau)$ be the softmax of the
confidence vector at round $r$ with temperature $\tau$ (we use $\tau=1$). ACE is the Shannon entropy of this distribution, normalised to
$[0,1]$ by its maximum:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ACE}(r) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(r) \log p_i(r),\quad
  \mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(r) = \mathrm{ACE}(r)\,/\,\log N.
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}\!=\!1$ when the distribution is flat. In this softmax
form it cannot approach $0$ on the stated domain: with $c_i\in[0,1]$ and $\tau=1$
no two softmax weights differ by more than a factor of $e$, so for five agents the value
stays in a narrow band just below $1$ (the pre-registration gives the band). The pre-registered tests therefore use the entropy
of the direct simplex projection $p_i = c_i/\sum_j c_j$ instead
(\S\ref{sec:probe}), which does reach $0$ when one agent holds all the confidence.

\paragraph{What ACE measures.} ACE is the entropy of a vector of stated
confidences normalised across agents. It is not the entropy of a predictive
distribution, and it is none of the total, expected (aleatoric) or
mutual-information (epistemic) terms of the ensemble decomposition of predictive
uncertainty. For the simplex projection, with $\mu$ the mean confidence,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}} = 1 - \frac{T}{\log N},\qquad
  T = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{c_i}{\mu}\log\frac{c_i}{\mu},
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the Theil index of the confidences: ACE is an \emph{evenness} index.
In the simplex form, it is unchanged when every confidence is multiplied by the same factor, so five
agents who all state a low confidence and five who all state a high one give the
same value, and it \emph{falls}
as the agents' confidences diverge. A flat distribution thus has two very
different causes --- \emph{confident consensus} and \emph{uniform uncertainty}
--- and ACE must be read alongside the mean confidence.

\subsection{Critique Severity Dispersion (CSD)}

Let $\mathbf{S}(r)$ collect the off-diagonal entries of the critique matrix.
We define CSD as the population variance of its entries:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CSD}(r) = \mathrm{Var}\left(\mathbf{S}(r)\right).
\end{equation}
Since $S_{ij}\in[0,1]$, $\mathrm{CSD}\in[0,\tfrac14]$. It is zero when all critiques
are equally severe, whatever their level, and largest when the severities split
evenly between $0$ and $1$: CSD measures how \emph{unequal} the critiques are, not
how severe. We also track the row- and column-variances
for diagnostic use (which critic is inconsistent? which proposal is contested?).

\subsection{Convergence signals}

For a run with $R$ rounds we track three convergence signals:
\begin{align}
  \Delta\mathrm{ACE}     &\triangleq \mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(R-1) - \mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(0), \\
  \Delta C               &\triangleq \overline{\mathbf{C}}(R-1) - \overline{\mathbf{C}}(0), \\
  \mathrm{WFR}           &\triangleq \frac{1}{R-1} \sum_{r=1}^{R-1} \mathbb{1}\!\left[w(r) \neq w(r-1)\right],
\end{align}
that is, last round minus first round, and the fraction of round transitions at
which the winner changes. The design expectation for a run that converges was
$\Delta C\!>\!0$ and $\mathrm{WFR}\!\approx\!0$. No sign of $\Delta\mathrm{ACE}$
follows from convergence: agreement among the agents raises evenness, and one
agent pulling ahead lowers it. We use $1-\min(\max(\Delta C,0),1)$ as a
bounded ``lack of convergence'' feature.

\paragraph{Undefined cases.} All three signals need $R\ge 2$. $\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}$
needs $N\ge 2$, and its simplex form needs a non-zero sum of confidences. In the
pre-registered tests a quantity that is undefined for a run is recorded as
undefined with its reason and is never imputed (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Task Difficulty Index}

We combine the four signals linearly:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{TDI} = \alpha\,\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(R-1)
               + \beta\,\mathrm{CSD}(R-1)
               + \gamma\,\bigl(1 - \min(\max(\Delta C, 0), 1)\bigr)
               + \delta\,\mathrm{WFR},
  \label{eq:tdi}
\end{equation}
with non-negative weights that sum to unity so $\mathrm{TDI}\!\in\![0,1]$. The
four terms are not on a common scale ($\mathrm{CSD}$ cannot exceed $\tfrac14$, and
in its softmax form $\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}$ occupies a narrow band near $1$),
so the weights are not relative importances and the index is a heuristic
composite, not a derived quantity. The hand-set \emph{default} weights are
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=(0.35,0.25,0.25,0.15)$. ``Calibrated'' in this paper
means \emph{fitted by ridge regression against held-out error}; it is not a
statement about probabilistic calibration, which the trace gives no label to
assess. Three forms of the index appear and should not be confused:
the four-term form~(\ref{eq:tdi}); the two-term lifecycle composite with
renormalised default weights that enters the ranking tests (\S\ref{sec:probe});
and the two-term ridge fit on standardised features that enters the transfer test
(\S\ref{subsec:h5}).

\section{Preflight Probe and Recommender}\label{sec:probe}

\paragraph{Forms used in the pre-registered tests.} For the pre-registered tests in
\S\ref{sec:results}, $\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}$ is the entropy of the \emph{direct
simplex projection} of the confidence vector, with no temperature, and the confidence-drop
term is the \emph{unclipped} $1-\Delta C\in[0,2]$; the lifecycle composite renormalises the
two default weights over these two terms and is not clipped, so
$\mathrm{TDI}_{\mathrm{lifecycle}}\in[0,17/12]$. The softmax form above and the clipped
term are reported descriptively beside them. Both changes were made by amendment before any
data from this sweep existed (Table~\ref{tab:amendments}).

\paragraph{Status of this section.} The probe and the recommender below are a
specification with released code. The recommender is not fitted on any data in
this paper and no result in \S\ref{sec:results} depends on it.

\paragraph{Probe signature.} Given a run, the round-$0$ probe signature is
\[\mathbf{x}_0 = (\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(0), \overline{\mathbf{C}}(0),
\max_i c_i(0), \mathrm{CSD}(0)) \in \mathbb{R}^4.\]
The released code computes the first entry in the softmax form. The probe is
meant to be produced by a \emph{shallow} one-round execution of the orchestrator
(smaller tool-call budget, narrower retrieval), cheaper than a full run; no such
shallow execution was run for this paper.

\paragraph{Recommender.} Let $\Phi$ be a discrete configuration
space of orchestration hyperparameters
$\varphi = (N, R, b)$. We model
$\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \varphi^\star \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\varphi,
\Sigma_\varphi)$ with diagonal $\Sigma_\varphi$, and weight each configuration by
$\pi(\varphi) \propto \exp(-\mathrm{cost}(\varphi) / \kappa)$, where
$\mathrm{cost}(\varphi) = N \cdot R \cdot \omega_b$ is a unitless proxy for compute
with a per-backbone weight $\omega_b$; it is not a count of floating-point
operations. $\pi$ sits where a prior would, but it encodes a cost preference, not
a belief about which configuration is best. Given a calibration set
$\{(\mathbf{x}_0^{(k)}, \varphi^{\star(k)})\}_{k=1}^K$, the recommender
picks
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\varphi(\mathbf{x}_0) = \arg\max_{\varphi \in \Phi,\, \mathrm{cost}(\varphi) \leq B}
  \;\; \log \pi(\varphi) + \log \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_0; \mu_\varphi, \Sigma_\varphi).
\end{equation}
This is a cost-penalised, plug-in Gaussian discriminant with independent features:
$\mu_\varphi$ and $\Sigma_\varphi$ are point estimates, no posterior over them or
over $\varphi$ is computed, and the rule returns a single configuration. We
therefore do not call it Bayesian in the text; the released class keeps the name
\texttt{BayesianRecommender}. Unfitted, as it is throughout this paper, the rule
ignores $\mathbf{x}_0$ and returns the cheapest configuration within the budget.
The default configuration space in the released code names backbones that are
not among those evaluated here (\S\ref{subsec:backbones}).

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\textbf{Procedure 1: probe-based hyperparameter recommendation (one task; specified, not evaluated).}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Input:} task $t$, calibrated means $\{\mu_\varphi\}$, variances
$\{\Sigma_\varphi\}$, budget $B$.\\
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,topsep=1pt,itemsep=0pt]
  \item $\mathbf{x}_0 \gets \textsc{Signature}(\textsc{ShallowOneRound}(t))$.
  \item For each $\varphi \in \Phi$ with $\mathrm{cost}(\varphi) \le B$:
        compute $\ell_\varphi = \log \pi(\varphi) + \log \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_0; \mu_\varphi, \Sigma_\varphi)$.
  \item Return $\arg\max_\varphi \ell_\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\section{Implementation and Observability Layer}\label{sec:impl}

So far the paper has been algorithmic. This section is deliberately
engineering-heavy because the paper's reviewer question
``\emph{what did you have to contribute to MassGen?}'' deserves a
precise answer. We separate our work into three buckets --- novel
contribution, thin adapter over existing infrastructure, and explicitly-scoped
future work --- and state each with enough detail that a reviewer can locate
the corresponding component in the released code (the file names are given in
\S\ref{sec:reproducibility}). A fuller design document is released with the code;
what follows is a one-section summary.

\subsection{The trace schema as the decoupling contract}

Our central engineering contribution is a narrow, immutable trace schema
that decouples the orchestrator from the evaluator. A per-round trace record
carries
\[(\text{round index}, \text{agent names}[N],
\mathbf{C}[N], \mathbf{S}[N,N{-}1], w, \text{consensus score},
\text{compute tokens});\]
a per-run trace record is an ordered tuple of per-round records plus the task
identifier, whether the run converged, and the backbone. Both are frozen
(immutable) Python dataclasses, which makes them safe to serialise,
hash, and pass across processes without defensive copying. The schema is
deliberately \emph{numeric-only}: metrics operate on the numbers alone ---
never on the LLM-facing proposal or critique text --- which makes metric
computation costs negligible (\textit{ca.}\ $1$ ms per run) and also
improves privacy posture (LLM content is never consumed by the metric layer).

\subsection{Adapting CellForge: duck typing, no patching}

The orchestrator returns a consensus result object whose rounds carry
proposals (the agent, its confidence and the content) and critiques (the
critic, the target agent and the severity). Our adapter is $\approx\!40$ lines of
pure projection: it duck-types those two interfaces, builds a critique
matrix that excludes self-critique, and reconstructs the per-round winner
as $\arg\max_j (c_j - \overline{S}_{\cdot j})$. No orchestrator patching is
required. Any propose-critique-vote system that exposes these two minimal
shapes gets the full evaluator stack for free.

\subsection{MassGen's native observability is rich but differently
shaped}\label{subsec:massgen_bridge}

\textsc{MassGen}~\citep{massgen2025github} already provides a mature
observability layer --- we are not claiming to add telemetry where there
is none. Its coordination tracker records each agent's answer (agent, content
and timestamp) and each vote (voter, the answer voted for, a reason and a
timestamp), and its execution trace emits structured markdown with round, vote
and tool-call entries.

Two structural differences prevent a direct wiring:
(i) a \textsc{MassGen} answer does not carry a numeric
confidence; confidence is \emph{implicit} in how many peer voters endorse
the answer.
(ii) the reason attached to a \textsc{MassGen} vote is a free-text rationale,
not a numeric severity.
\textsc{MassGen}'s own contributor guidelines explicitly forbid
keyword/regex-based categorisation of such rationales (\emph{``No
keyword/heuristic matching for categorization or similarity''}), so the
projection must use a small LLM-based rater.

We therefore specify \textbf{a two-step extractor}, drafted in the
repository:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \emph{Vote-share confidence.} For each round, agent $j$'s projected
        confidence is $\hat{c}_j = v_j/(N{-}1)$, where $v_j$ is the number of
        votes agent $j$ receives. This
        is deterministic and requires no LLM call.
  \item \emph{LLM-rated severity.} For each vote, project the free-text
        reason onto a severity score in $[0,1]$ via a terse rater
        prompt, released with the code.
        The extractor caches by reason string so at most one LLM call per
        distinct reason per session.
\end{enumerate}

The corresponding \textsc{MassGen} skill declares two hooks: one at the start of
a session runs the preflight probe, and one at its end runs the evaluation. Both
handlers are $\leq 20$ lines of glue over the evaluator's JSON entry points, which
keeps the skill minimal and audit-friendly.

\paragraph{What we have \emph{not} done.} We have not (a) submitted the
skill to \textsc{MassGen} upstream, (b) calibrated the severity rater
against human annotators on recorded \textsc{MassGen} traces, or (c) validated
the vote-share confidence projection against an alternative projection
(e.g.\ an LLM-rated confidence). Each of these is one incremental
engineering step and we flag them here so the boundary between our paper's
claims and ``plug it in and it works'' is unambiguous.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why the question is posed as a question.} The title asks whether
multi-agent confidence predicts task difficulty or only reacts to it, and asserts
neither, so that a null answer is publishable as it
stands. As the pre-registration requires, a null or unsupported outcome on the
trace-feature ranking or the cross-dataset transfer hypothesis is reported as the
answer and is not traded back into a claim-form title, abstract or contribution
list. The title was amended twice after the outcome was known, by the fifth and
sixth amendments (Table~\ref{tab:amendments}). The pre-registered title named agent
confidence entropy, singular. The fifth amendment named the group's distribution, the
object of the narrowed claim, and reported both outcomes of the ranking test in its
subtitle. The sixth was made after the error feedback behind the ranking outcome was
found (\S\ref{sec:setup_exp}): the title now also asks whether confidence only reacts
to difficulty, and it reports no outcome. The question form is unchanged throughout.
The fifth amendment also renames the
pre-registration's ``gate'' to ``pre-registered criterion''; no hypothesis,
threshold or outcome changes with the word. The sixth also records that the
exploratory analyses of \S\ref{subsec:exploratory} were added after the outcome.

\paragraph{Reading the criteria together.} The two attainable-error hypotheses
bound what the trainer sweep can reach (an oracle, \S\ref{subsec:oracle}); they say
nothing about whether the agents find those configurations. The backbone-choice
hypothesis asks whether the Architect uses its choice of backbone at all, and the
ranking and transfer hypotheses ask whether the trace metrics carry information
about difficulty. Only those last two bear on the title question. On this run the
first three hypotheses are supported. The
ranking hypothesis is supported through the confidence-drop component
($1-\Delta C$) and through the composite that inherits it, in both screens, while
confidence entropy runs opposite to the hypothesis: its two tests do not meet the
pre-registered one-sided criterion, and given the narrow observed spread of
$\mathrm{ACE}_{\mathrm{norm}}$ the composite $\mathrm{TDI}_{\mathrm{lifecycle}}$
ranks tasks nearly identically to $1-\Delta C$. The transfer hypothesis is not
supported: the composite fitted on Adamson reaches $\rho=\resHFiveRho$ on Norman
against a criterion of $\resHFiveThreshold$, with an interval that contains both
zero and the criterion. On this evidence, the group's confidence entropy does not
rank tasks in the pre-registered direction; its confidence drop across rounds ranks
tasks by held-out error within each screen; and the composite fitted on Adamson did
not meet the transfer criterion on Norman. The negative point estimates are
reported, not interpreted, and a directional hypothesis about them would need a new
pre-registration.

\paragraph{How the pre-registered pass is to be read.} The title asks
whether multi-agent confidence \emph{predicts} task difficulty or only reacts to it. The
pre-registered ranking criterion is met, and that outcome stands. But the feature
that meets it, $1-\Delta C$, compares confidence stated after the agents were shown
the previous round's held-out error with confidence stated before
(\S\ref{sec:setup_exp}), and the rank order of tasks by error is nearly the same
from round to round. The exploratory analysis of \S\ref{subsec:exploratory} finds no
rank association between the pre-feedback mean confidence and error, and a strong one
once the error has been shown. Read with the design and that analysis, the
pre-registered pass is consistent with a \emph{reactive} reading: agents lower their
stated confidence more where a larger measured error was put in front of them. On
that reading it is a check that the agents respond to their feedback. It is not
evidence of prediction in the sense the allocation problem needs, because the feature
exists only after a full train-and-evaluate round on the held-out cells, which is the
compute an allocator would be trying to save. The reactive reading is itself not
established as causal (\S\ref{sec:limits}). Whether confidence elicited without
the error in context carries any signal is not answered by this run; it needs a
new pre-registered experiment.

\paragraph{What a single-round probe can and cannot observe.} A single-round
probe by definition cannot observe convergence. It sees only the starting
distribution of agent confidences and critiques, which encodes a snapshot of
prior agreement but not the response-to-critique dynamics that $\Delta C$ and
WFR summarise. That is a structural property of the minimal probe. The data now
add an empirical observation, exploratory and stated as such: in this run the
round-$0$ mean confidence, which is what such a probe would see, does not rank
tasks by error (\S\ref{subsec:exploratory}), and the probe and recommender of
\S\ref{sec:probe} were not evaluated.

\paragraph{Implications for upstream systems.} For
\textsc{CellForge}~\citep{chen2025cellforge} and
\textsc{MassGen}~\citep{massgen2025github}, one recommendation does not depend
on the criterion outcomes: \emph{emit the $\{C(r), S(r), w(r)\}$ trace, and the
serving model of every step, as first-class outputs} of every orchestrator
run. Without them neither difficulty metrics nor the attribution of agent
choices to a model is recoverable after the fact (Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}).

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Backbone scope.} \begin{sloppypar}The backbones are small models trained per task
(\S\ref{subsec:backbones}); no pretrained single-cell foundation model is
evaluated. Integrating pretrained \textsc{scGPT}~\citep{cui2024scgpt},
\textsc{Geneformer}~\citep{theodoris2023geneformer}, or
\textsc{scFoundation} is the most consequential follow-up.\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Oracle, not held-out, error.} The attainable-error hypotheses select the
best of \resNDistinctConfigs{} distinct configurations (\resNTrainerRecordsPerTask{}
records per task) on the same evaluation genes and cells they are scored on
(\S\ref{subsec:oracle}). There is no nested validation/test split; the
quantity is an attainable upper bound.

\paragraph{Evaluation genes and feature spaces.} The top-$20$ differentially
expressed genes are chosen using the held-out cells (the CPA/GEARS convention), and
each task's HVG set is selected on that task's training cells, so the cross-task
medians aggregate models with different inputs (\S\ref{subsec:eval}).

\paragraph{LLM condition: a single model family.} All five roles are served
by Anthropic Claude models, as amended before data collection, so nothing here
supports a claim of cross-family generality, and the backbone-choice entropy may be
lower than a multi-family pool would give. The Validator judges proposals from the
same family; it sits on a different tier (Sonnet 5.5) from the four Haiku 4.5
proposer roles, which is reported, not a remedy --- residual same-family judge
bias is acknowledged. Model identity is recorded and asserted per call.

\paragraph{The passing feature is conditioned on a shown error.} From round $1$ on,
every role's prompt carries the previous round's held-out MSD (\S\ref{par:feedback}).
The confidence drop that meets the ranking criterion is therefore stated after an
error measured on the same evaluation genes as the target has been shown. The
later-round prompts carry more than that error. The Architect's prompt adds the
rule-based adjustment, failed-gene labels and the Literature genes. The prompts of the
other four roles add those and a one-line rationale holding the held-out sign
agreement and the accept-or-reject verdict against the Validator's threshold. The
round index changes with them as well. The design therefore does not isolate the
shown error as the cause of the drop. The pre-registered null (no association) does
not model this feedback, the pre-registration did not state it, and the archived
versions of this paper before this one did not disclose it. A test of prediction
proper would elicit confidence with no error in context; that needs new runs under a
new pre-registration.

\paragraph{Point-estimate criteria, no power analysis, one sweep.} Every criterion
thresholds a point estimate at about twenty tasks per screen, and no power analysis
was made. The intervals of the tests that meet the ranking criterion extend below
the criterion value, and the transfer interval contains both zero and its criterion
(\S\ref{sec:results}). Tasks within a screen share control cells, a training pool,
the models and the prompts, while the bootstrap and the null treat them as
exchangeable. LLM sampling is not seeded, and the study is a single sweep with
three within-sweep seeds per task and no replicate sweep. The tally of supported
hypotheses counts two feasibility checks and one check of a design lever alongside
the two hypotheses that bear on the title question.

\paragraph{Sample size and the sign of confidence entropy.} The within-screen tests
use $n=\resHFourAdaAceN$ and $n=\resHFourNorAceN$ tasks; every $\rho$ carries a
bootstrap CI over tasks (Table~\ref{tab:tdi-real}), and the pre-registered
false-positive rate of the six-test criterion under the null is a few percent. The
point estimates for confidence entropy were negative in both screens (the Adamson CI
excludes zero, the Norman CI includes it); that direction was not pre-registered,
generates no claim, and would need its own pre-registration to test. On Norman the
oracle median of the doublet stratum (\resHTwoDoubletMedian) lies above the
attainable-error criterion while the median over all tasks meets it; the stratum is
reported, as pre-registered.

\paragraph{Cell-line and dataset scope.} Both datasets are K562, and the
Architect's backbone-choice distribution is K562-conditioned. Genome-scale
validation on \textsc{Replogle 2022}~\citep{replogle2022perturbseq} and a
non-K562 follow-up are explicit next steps.

\paragraph{Configuration space.} $|\Phi|$ remains modest. Widening to
include continuous learning-rate schedules, per-agent retrieval depth,
and explicit batch-correction ablations is straightforward but would
expand the trainer sweep beyond its budget cap.

\paragraph{The probe and recommender are not evaluated.} The recommender of
\S\ref{sec:probe} is never fitted in this paper, so nothing here shows that a
round-$0$ signature can select a configuration. It is a plug-in point rule and
reports no uncertainty.

\paragraph{Reward model free-riding.} We deliberately do not train a
process reward model. A PRM over agent traces could outperform hand-designed
metrics, but it would also require an expensive labelling phase. The
question tested here is whether hand-designed difficulty metrics over the
already-produced trace carry the signal; a PRM is a complementary rather
than competing direction.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proposed ACE, CSD, $\Delta C$-based convergence signals, and WFR as a
four-feature lens on multi-agent group-generation traces, combined into a
Task Difficulty Index, and proposed the TDI and its probe as a candidate
multi-agent analogue of \citet{snell2024scaling}'s compute-optimal
single-chain allocation. We test whether these signals rank tasks by held-out
error under five pre-registered criteria on $41$ held-out K562 tasks, with
every run carrying its revision, configuration, dataset digests, task plan,
label contract and per-step serving model. The test gives this answer:
\resGateSummary. Within each screen the change in confidence across rounds, and the
composite that inherits it, rank tasks by held-out error at the pre-registered level;
confidence entropy does not, in the pre-registered direction; and the
fitted composite did not meet the transfer criterion across screens. The
confidence change that meets its criterion is stated after the agents have been
shown a held-out error, and in an exploratory analysis the mean confidence stated
before any feedback does not rank tasks. Read with the design and that analysis,
the pre-registered pass is consistent with the group's stated confidence
responding to measured error; it is not evidence that confidence predicts
difficulty before that error is measured. That question remains open, and the
probe and recommender that would use such a signal were not evaluated.

\paragraph{Code and data.} All artifacts are released under Apache-2.0 at the
repository that accompanies this submission, including the
\textsc{MassGen}-installable skill that produces per-run metric reports for
any propose-critique-vote orchestrator.
