\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup_exp}

\subsection{Data and provenance}\label{subsec:datasets}

We use two published K562 Perturb-seq screens in the scPerturb
repackaging~\citep{peidli2024scperturb} (Zenodo record $13350497$):
\textsc{Adamson 2016}~\citep{adamson2016perturbseq}, a CRISPR interference
screen, from its three 10X runs (pilot, 10X005, 10X010) concatenated onto the
intersection of their gene vocabularies; and
\textsc{Norman 2019}~\citep{norman2019perturbseq}, a CRISPR activation screen
with single and double perturbations. All four files are fetched
programmatically; each file's SHA-256 digest is pinned in
\texttt{src/perturb\_eval/data/download.py}, the digests were cross-checked
against the size and MD5 published in the Zenodo record, and a missing pin or
a digest mismatch refuses the fetch. Perturbation labels follow a recorded
label contract. Adamson raw construct labels are parsed structurally: the
plasmid suffix and \texttt{\_only} are stripped; a one-component construct
becomes a gene task (plasmids for the same gene are pooled and the guide
count per gene recorded); multi-gene constructs are excluded; negative-control
constructs are controls; cells with a missing perturbation annotation are
excluded and counted; and labels whose target cannot be corroborated in the
data are excluded with the reason. Norman symbols renamed since publication
are joined to the dataset vocabulary on their Ensembl gene IDs, and labels
that resolve under neither name are excluded with the reason. The held-out
task plan is drawn deterministically: Adamson targets are binned into $3$
quantile bins of mean $|\Delta\log1\mathrm{p}|$ on the target gene and up to $7$
are drawn per bin from the structurally eligible single-gene constructs with
\texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng(2026)}; a bin with fewer eligible labels
contributes all of them, and the draw is topped up to exactly $21$ by a seeded
permutation of the sorted remaining labels (or trimmed to the sorted prefix).
Norman singletons and doublets are drawn separately, stratified by a CRC32-derived
stratum and topped up or trimmed to exactly $15$ and $5$ by the same rule ($20$
tasks), for $41$ tasks in total. Only the per-pool total is asserted, with an
explicit \texttt{raise}; a pool smaller than its requested count refuses the run
in preflight, and the per-bin eligible-pool sizes are written to provenance so an
uneven fill is visible (amendment~2, A2-7). Every excluded label, every digest, the
resolved task plan with its strata and eligible-pool sizes, and the per-label
cell cap (\texttt{max\_cells\_per\_pert}) are written to the run's
provenance record (\S\ref{sec:reproducibility}).

\paragraph{On-target signal.} Each backbone receives the signed
log-fold-change of the perturbation's target gene(s) as its on-target
feature; for a doublet it is the mean over the two target genes. In Adamson
(CRISPR interference) the on-target signal is a decrease; in Norman (CRISPR
activation) it is an increase. Because the feature is signed, the direction
is learned from the data rather than assumed.

\subsection{Features and evaluation}\label{subsec:eval}

\paragraph{Features.} For each held-out task, highly variable genes are selected on that
task's \emph{training} cells only; the trainer sweep uses $2000$, and in the lifecycle the
count is the agents' applied choice (amendment~2, A2-3), so feature spaces may vary by run as
well as by task, and every cross-task median is a median over models with differing inputs.
The target gene(s) and the $20$ evaluation genes are force-included in every feature set.
In this run every applied choice was \resHVGAppliedValue{} (\resHVGDistinctApplied{} distinct
value over \resNRoundsTotal{} rounds; HVG-count entropy \resHVGEntropy{} nats), so the lifecycle
and trainer feature sets coincide up to the forced genes.

\paragraph{Evaluation genes.} The error metric is the mean squared deviation (MSD) between
predicted and observed mean log-fold-change on the $20$ genes with the largest absolute mean
difference between the held-out perturbation's cells and control cells --- the CPA/GEARS
convention~\citep{lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2024gears}. These genes are chosen once per task
on the full post-QC gene axis and shared by the trainer and lifecycle paths (A2-5); the
analyser refuses a run in which the two paths' lists differ. They are chosen using the
held-out cells.

\subsection{Backbone pool}\label{subsec:backbones}

The Architect picks from three backbones: \textsc{linear} (ridge regression
per gene, NumPy); \textsc{mlp} (a two-layer MLP, PyTorch); and a
$2.1$\,M-parameter gene-token transformer trained from scratch (code
identifier \texttt{scgpt\_small}; not a pretrained foundation model).

\subsection{Trainer sweep and the oracle quantity}\label{subsec:oracle}

For every task the trainer sweep fits $\{\textsc{linear}, \textsc{mlp}, \texttt{scgpt\_small}\}
\times R\in\{1,2,3\} \times 3$ seeds ($2026$--$2028$): \resNTrainerRecordsPerTask{} records per
task. The \textsc{linear} backbone ignores both $R$ and the seed, so the grid holds
\resNDistinctConfigs{} distinct $(\text{backbone}, R)$ configurations
(\resNDistinctFits{} distinct fits including seeds); the counts are recorded in provenance
(amendments~2 and 3: $N$ was removed as an axis, and the stated count is the distinct
$(\text{backbone}, R)$ count). The quantity gated in H1/H2 is, per task, the minimum MSD over
every finite record, then its median over tasks. The minimum is selected on
the same evaluation genes and cells it is scored on, so it is an
\emph{oracle}: an attainable upper bound on what the configuration space can
reach, not an estimate of held-out performance. There is no nested
validation/test split.

\subsection{Agentic lifecycle}\label{subsec:lifecycle}

Five agents --- DataCurator, Literature, Architect, Trainer, Validator --- produce
Pydantic-validated proposals. Every proposal states a verbalised \texttt{confidence} in $[0,1]$;
a missing, non-numeric, non-finite or out-of-range value is a schema failure, the step falls back and the
run is invalid --- confidence is never imputed (amendment~2, A2-1). Every run has exactly three
rounds with no early stop, and the Validator's verdict never stops the run (A2-2). Its
required threshold \texttt{dynamic\_threshold\_msd} $\in[0.02,0.3]$ decides whether the
round's fit is accepted, and that decision selects the rule-based
\texttt{score\_and\_gate} critique delta applied at the top precedence tier in the next
round (A3-3); ``Validator delta'' below means that rule-based delta, while the LLM
Validator's own \texttt{critique.suggested\_next\_config\_delta} is recorded and
\emph{not} applied (A3-2). In this run the threshold was stated on all
\resNValidatorSteps{} Validator steps (accepted \resValidatorAccepted, rejected
\resValidatorRejected), and a Validator-sourced value was applied in
\resValidatorSourcedRounds{} of \resNRoundsTotal{} rounds.
The executors \emph{apply} the parsed fields --- the HVG count, learning rate, ridge $\lambda$
and epochs --- with precedence Validator delta $>$ Architect $>$ DataCurator $>$ Trainer $>$
defaults, and the applied value and its source are recorded per round (A2-3, A3-2); the
DataCurator's mito-QC threshold is recorded but not applied (no per-cell mito fraction is
loaded; A3-1). The Architect's \texttt{backbone} is required and must be on the pinned menu
$\{$\textsc{linear}, \textsc{mlp}, \texttt{scgpt\_small}$\}$; each Architect step records the
backbone it \emph{stated} and the backbone that \emph{ran} after the Validator's rotation rule,
and H3 gates on the stated backbone with the executed one reported alongside (A2-6). The
Literature role is an LLM prompt with no retrieval tools; its expected up/down-regulated genes
are passed to the next round's Architect prompt. Fields the agents may state but no executor
applies (\texttt{n\_agents}, \texttt{n\_rounds}, \texttt{split\_strategy},
\texttt{batch\_correction}, \texttt{hvg\_method}) are recorded only.

\paragraph{LLM condition (amendment~4).} The agents are served through the Anthropic Messages
API by a pinned roster: \texttt{\resRosterHaiku} for DataCurator, Literature, Architect and
Trainer, and \texttt{\resRosterSonnet} for the Validator, so the judge sits on a different tier
from the roles it judges; each role fails over to the other model. Every reply is constrained
to the role's JSON schema by structured outputs (numeric ranges are enforced by the role schema
after parsing, never clamped); the two free-form maps in the Literature and Validator
schemas travel as key/value arrays and are rebuilt after parsing (A4-1). Every prompt
states the dataset and its modality (A2-8). Thinking is off on both models (Haiku 4.5: none; Sonnet 5.5:
\texttt{between\_tools} at low effort). Sampling: Haiku at temperature $0.3$ (the value the earlier OpenRouter roster used) and
Sonnet 5.5 at API defaults, which rejects non-default sampling. Per-role output ceilings ($4\times$ the dry run's observed maximum, minimum 256):
DataCurator \resCeilingCurator, Literature \resCeilingLiterature, Architect \resCeilingArchitect,
Trainer \resCeilingTrainer, Validator \resCeilingValidator; a \texttt{max\_tokens} stop is retried
once at twice the ceiling, both billed, and a second is a fallback-class event. Every call records
its \texttt{stop\_reason}, the requested and the served model (asserted equal --- a mismatch is a
fallback-class event with no failover), and its token usage; a refusal aborts the run; the request
never carries a server-side fallback parameter; 401/402/403 abort. Preflight probes both roster
models with every role's schema before any spend and records the liveness table in provenance.
Each lifecycle step is labelled \texttt{source} $\in\{\texttt{llm},\texttt{fallback}\}$ with its
\texttt{model\_id}; a run with any fallback step is invalid, and the pre-registered run starts on
an empty, version-namespaced LLM cache so that no reply is replayed (A2-8; cache entries at
start \resCacheStart, hits \resCacheHits). Each task runs the lifecycle with three seeds; the seed
is passed to the trainer and into the LLM cache key. Every call in this run was served by its role's pinned model (failovers \resNFailover;
Validator calls on \texttt{\resRoleServedValidator}: \resRoleCallsValidator), and the preflight
liveness table recorded \resLivenessOkPairs{} of \resLivenessTotalPairs{} model--role probes
live. Two caveats are stated here, as A4-3 requires, and taken up in \S\ref{sec:limits}: every role runs on a single model family, so nothing here supports a
cross-family claim; and the judge is same-family on a different tier, which is reported,
not a remedy.

\paragraph{What the lifecycle trace records.} Each step records its role, round, proposal, LLM
confidence, \texttt{source}, \texttt{model\_id}, \texttt{stop\_reason} and served model; Architect
steps record stated and executed backbones; Validator steps the verdict and threshold. The five
roles do not critique each other, and no round has a winner, so CSD, WFR and the four-component
TDI of \S\ref{sec:metrics} cannot be computed on this testbed; H4 and H5 use only the
confidence-derived quantities (\S\ref{sec:tdi-real}).

\subsection{Compute}\label{subsec:compute}

Preflight, trainer sweep and lifecycle sweep run in one process on a single Modal A100-40GB
container. Spend is measured as GPU wall-clock plus LLM usage at the pinned list prices plus
the spend carried in from an aborted earlier attempt and the dry run, against pre-registered lines of
\$\resSpendStopLine{} (stop and report) and \$\resKillLine{} (kill) under a \$\resCeilingLine{}
ceiling (A4-2); the lines are limits, not the spend. Every run is appended atomically to
\texttt{trainer\_runs.jsonl} or \texttt{lifecycle\_runs.jsonl}, whose record~0 is the run's
provenance record. Actual spend: LLM \$\resSpendLLM, GPU \$\resSpendGPU{} (\resGPUHours{}
A100-hours), prior \$\resSpendPrior; total \$\resSpendTotal, neither line reached. The pre-registered
projection (A4-2) was \$\resProjTotal{} in total (LLM \$\resProjLLM, GPU \$\resProjGPU{} at
\resProjLatency{}\,s of LLM latency per round while the GPU is held, prior
\$\resSpendPrior); the LLM and GPU components both came in above the projection, within
the stop line.
