\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup_exp}

The design described here is the pre-registered one, including the amendments made before any
data were collected; Table~\ref{tab:amendments} lists them.

\subsection{Data and provenance}\label{subsec:datasets}

We use two published K562 Perturb-seq screens in the scPerturb
repackaging~\citep{peidli2024scperturb} (Zenodo record $13350497$):
\textsc{Adamson 2016}~\citep{adamson2016perturbseq}, a CRISPR interference
screen, from its three 10X runs (pilot, 10X005, 10X010) concatenated onto the
intersection of their gene vocabularies; and
\textsc{Norman 2019}~\citep{norman2019perturbseq}, a CRISPR activation screen
with single and double perturbations. All four files are fetched
programmatically. The SHA-256 digest of each file is pinned in the code and was
cross-checked against the size and MD5 published in the Zenodo record; a missing
pin or a digest mismatch refuses the fetch.

Perturbation labels follow a recorded label contract. Adamson construct labels are
parsed structurally, after the plasmid suffix and the ``only'' marker are removed. A
construct that targets one gene becomes a gene task, which pools all plasmids for that gene
and records the guide count. Constructs that target several genes are excluded, and
negative-control constructs serve as controls. Cells with no perturbation annotation are
excluded and counted, and so are labels whose target cannot be corroborated in the data, with
the reason recorded. Norman symbols renamed since publication are joined to the dataset vocabulary on
their Ensembl gene IDs, and labels that resolve under neither name are excluded with the
reason.

The held-out task plan is drawn deterministically. Adamson targets are binned into $3$
quantile bins of the mean $|\Delta\log1\mathrm{p}|$ of the target gene, and up to $7$ are
drawn per bin from the structurally eligible single-gene constructs with a generator seeded
with 2026. A bin with fewer eligible labels contributes all of them, and the draw is then
topped up to exactly $21$ by a seeded permutation of the sorted remaining labels, or trimmed
to the sorted prefix. Norman singletons and doublets are drawn separately, stratified by strata
derived from a CRC32 hash, and topped up or trimmed by the same rule to exactly $15$
and $5$ ($20$ tasks), for $41$ tasks in total. Only the per-pool total is asserted: a pool
smaller than its requested count refuses the run in preflight, and the eligible-pool size of
each bin is written to the provenance record so that an uneven fill is visible. Every excluded label, every digest, the resolved task plan with
its strata and eligible-pool sizes, and the cap on cells per perturbation label are written
to the run's provenance record (\S\ref{sec:reproducibility}).

\paragraph{On-target signal.} Each backbone receives the signed
log-fold-change of the perturbation's target gene(s) as its on-target
feature; for a doublet it is the mean over the two target genes. In Adamson
(CRISPR interference) the on-target signal is a decrease; in Norman (CRISPR
activation) it is an increase. Because the feature is signed, the direction
is learned from the data rather than assumed.

\subsection{Features and evaluation}\label{subsec:eval}

\paragraph{Features.} For each held-out task, highly variable genes (HVGs) are selected on
that task's \emph{training} cells only. The trainer sweep uses $2000$; in the lifecycle the
count is the value the agents chose and the executor applied. Feature spaces may therefore vary by run as well as
by task, and every cross-task median is a median over models with differing inputs. The
target gene(s) and the $20$ evaluation genes are force-included in every feature set. In
this run every applied choice was \resHVGAppliedValue{}, a single distinct value
(\resHVGDistinctApplied) over \resNRoundsTotal{} rounds, so the entropy of the HVG count is
\resHVGEntropy{} nats and the lifecycle and trainer feature sets coincide up to the forced genes.

\paragraph{Evaluation genes.} The error metric is the mean squared deviation (MSD) between
predicted and observed mean log-fold-change on the $20$ genes with the largest absolute mean
difference between the held-out perturbation's cells and control cells, the CPA/GEARS
convention~\citep{lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2024gears}. These genes are chosen once per task
on the full gene axis after quality control (QC) and are shared by the trainer sweep and the agentic lifecycle;
the analysis refuses a run in which the two lists differ. They are chosen using the
held-out cells.

\subsection{Backbone pool}\label{subsec:backbones}

The Architect picks from three backbones: ridge regression fitted separately for each gene
in NumPy (\textsc{linear}), a two-layer multilayer perceptron in PyTorch (\textsc{mlp}), and a
$2.1$\,M-parameter gene-token transformer trained from scratch, which is not a pretrained
foundation model.

\subsection{Trainer sweep and the oracle quantity}\label{subsec:oracle}

For every task the trainer sweep fits each of the three backbones with $R\in\{1,2,3\}$ and
$3$ seeds ($2026$--$2028$): \resNTrainerRecordsPerTask{} records per task. The
\textsc{linear} backbone ignores both $R$ and the seed, so the grid holds
\resNDistinctConfigs{} distinct $(\text{backbone}, R)$ configurations
(\resNDistinctFits{} distinct fits including seeds); the counts are recorded in provenance.
Two amendments made before data collection removed the agent count $N$ as an axis and
defined the stated count as the number of distinct $(\text{backbone}, R)$ configurations. The
quantity tested against the attainable-error criteria is,
per task, the minimum MSD over every finite record, and then its median over tasks. The
minimum is selected on the same evaluation genes and cells it is scored on, so it is an
\emph{oracle}: an attainable upper bound on what the configuration space can reach, not an
estimate of held-out performance. There is no nested validation/test split.

\subsection{Agentic lifecycle}\label{subsec:lifecycle}

Five agents (DataCurator, Literature, Architect, Trainer and Validator) produce proposals that
are validated against a schema. Every proposal states a verbalised confidence in $[0,1]$. A
missing, non-numeric, non-finite or out-of-range value is a schema failure: the step falls
back to the rule-based default and the run is invalid, so confidence is never imputed. Every
run has exactly three rounds with no early stop, and the Validator's verdict never stops the
run.

The Validator must state an acceptance threshold on the MSD, in $[0.02,0.3]$. The threshold
decides whether the round's fit is accepted, and that decision selects a rule-based
adjustment to the configuration, which is applied at the highest precedence in the next
round; ``Validator delta'' below means that rule-based adjustment. The LLM Validator's own
suggested adjustment is recorded and \emph{not} applied. In this run the threshold was stated
on all \resNValidatorSteps{} Validator steps (\resValidatorAccepted{} accepted and
\resValidatorRejected{} rejected), and a value from the Validator was applied in
\resValidatorSourcedRounds{} of \resNRoundsTotal{} rounds.

The executors \emph{apply} the parsed fields (the HVG count, learning rate, ridge $\lambda$
and number of epochs) with precedence Validator delta $>$ Architect $>$ DataCurator $>$ Trainer $>$
defaults, and the applied value and its source are recorded per round. The DataCurator's
threshold for the QC of mitochondrial reads is recorded but not applied, because no per-cell
mitochondrial fraction is loaded. The Architect must state a backbone from the fixed menu of
three; each Architect step records the backbone it \emph{stated} and the backbone that
\emph{ran} after the Validator's rotation rule, and the backbone-choice criterion is evaluated
on the stated backbone, with the executed one reported alongside. The Literature role is an
LLM prompt with no retrieval tools; its expected up- and down-regulated genes are passed to
the Architect's prompt in the next round. Fields that the agents may state but no executor
applies (the number of agents and rounds, the split strategy, the batch correction and the
HVG method) are recorded only. The precedence order, the unapplied mitochondrial-read
threshold and the use of the stated backbone were fixed by amendments made before data
collection.

\paragraph{What each agent has seen when it states a confidence.}\label{par:feedback}
Within a round, all five roles state their proposals and confidences \emph{before} that
round's model is fitted and scored. In round $0$ the prompts carry the task and the dataset and
no result. From round $1$ on, every role's prompt also carries the previous round's outcome:
the held-out MSD on the evaluation genes, the Validator's verdict on it, the rule-based
adjustment, the genes that failed and the Literature genes. The confidences of rounds $1$ and $2$
are therefore stated after the agents have been shown a held-out error, measured on the same
evaluation genes on which the final error is scored; only the round-$0$ confidences are stated
before any error feedback. The confidence-drop feature $\Delta C$ is the last round's mean
confidence minus the first round's, so it is a difference between confidence after that
feedback and confidence before it. This is how the lifecycle was designed and run; it was not
stated in the pre-registration or in the earlier archived versions of this paper, and it
governs how the ranking result is read (\S\ref{sec:tdi-real}, \S\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Large language model condition.} The agents are large language models (LLMs),
served through the Anthropic Messages API by a pinned roster adopted before data collection
(Table~\ref{tab:amendments}): Claude Haiku 4.5 (\resRosterHaiku) for DataCurator, Literature,
Architect and Trainer, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (\resRosterSonnet) for the Validator,
so the judge sits on a different tier from the roles it judges. Each role fails over to the
other model. Every reply is constrained to the role's JSON schema by structured outputs, and
numeric ranges are enforced by the role schema after parsing, never clamped. The two free-form
maps in the Literature and Validator schemas are sent as key/value arrays and rebuilt after
parsing. Every prompt states the dataset and its modality. As recorded, extended thinking was \resThinkingHaiku{} for Haiku 4.5 and
\resThinkingSonnet{} for Sonnet 5.5, at \resEffortSonnet{} effort. Haiku was sampled at temperature \resTemperatureHaiku{}, the value
used by an earlier roster, and Sonnet 5.5 at its \resSamplingSonnet, because it rejects
non-default sampling. The output ceiling of each role is four times the largest output seen in
the dry run, with a minimum of 256 tokens: DataCurator \resCeilingCurator, Literature
\resCeilingLiterature, Architect \resCeilingArchitect, Trainer \resCeilingTrainer{} and
Validator \resCeilingValidator. A reply that stops at its ceiling is retried once at twice the
ceiling, with both calls billed, and a second such stop is treated like a fallback. In this
run, the calls that reached their ceiling (\resStopMaxTokens{} of \resNCalls) were each retried
once at twice the ceiling and then ended normally, as did the other \resStopEndTurn.

Every call records why it stopped, the requested and the served model, which are asserted
equal (a mismatch is treated like a fallback, with no failover), and its token usage. A
refusal aborts the run, as does an authentication, payment, permission or not-found error
(HTTP 401/402/403/404), and no request asks the API for a server-side fallback model.
Before any spend, the preflight probes both roster models with every role's schema and records
the liveness table in provenance. Each lifecycle step is labelled as coming from the LLM or
from the rule-based fallback, together with the model that served it; a run with any fallback
step is invalid, and the pre-registered run starts on an empty LLM cache, specific to its
version, so that no reply is replayed (\resCacheStart{} cache entries at the start and
\resCacheHits{} hits). Each task runs the lifecycle with three seeds, and the seed is passed to
the trainer and into the LLM cache key. Every call in this run was served by its role's pinned
model, with \resNFailover{} failovers; all \resRoleCallsValidator{} Validator calls were served
by \resRoleServedValidator. The preflight liveness table recorded \resLivenessOkPairs{} of
\resLivenessTotalPairs{} model--role probes as live. The pre-registration requires two caveats
to be stated here; both are taken up in \S\ref{sec:limits}. Every role runs on a
single model family, so nothing here supports a cross-family claim; and the judge is same-family on a
different tier, which is reported, not a remedy.

\paragraph{What the lifecycle trace records.} Each step records its role, round, proposal, LLM
confidence, source (LLM or fallback), serving model, reason for stopping and served model;
Architect steps record the stated and the executed backbone, and Validator steps the verdict
and the threshold. The five roles do not critique each other, and no round has a winner, so
CSD, WFR and the four-component TDI of \S\ref{sec:metrics} cannot be computed on this testbed.
The trace-feature ranking and cross-dataset transfer hypotheses therefore use only the
confidence-derived quantities (\S\ref{sec:tdi-real}).

\subsection{Compute}\label{subsec:compute}

Preflight, trainer sweep and lifecycle sweep run in one process on a single Modal A100-40GB
container. Spend is measured as GPU wall-clock time plus LLM usage at the pinned list prices
plus the spend carried in from an aborted earlier attempt and the dry run. It is held against
pre-registered lines of \$\resSpendStopLine{} (stop and report) and \$\resKillLine{} (kill)
under a \$\resCeilingLine{} ceiling; the lines are limits, not the spend. Every run is
appended atomically to the trainer or the lifecycle record file, whose first record is the
run's provenance record. Actual spend was \$\resSpendLLM{} on LLM calls, \$\resSpendGPU{} on
GPU time (\resGPUHours{} A100-hours) and \$\resSpendPrior{} carried in, a total of
\$\resSpendTotal, and neither line was reached. The pre-registered projection, made with the
spend lines before data collection (Table~\ref{tab:amendments}), was \$\resProjTotal{} in total:
\$\resProjLLM{} for LLM calls and \$\resProjGPU{} for GPU time, at \resProjLatency{}\,s of LLM
latency per round while the GPU is held, plus the \$\resSpendPrior{} carried in. The LLM and GPU
components both came in above the projection, within the stop line.
