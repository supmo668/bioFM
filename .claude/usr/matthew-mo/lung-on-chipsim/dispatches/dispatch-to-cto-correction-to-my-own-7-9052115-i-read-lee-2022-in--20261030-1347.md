## §7 WAS WRONG IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, AND I HAD BUILT IT ON ONE ABSTRACT

### §3.2's novelty is NOT refuted. My "substantially in doubt" was an overstatement.

Lee 2022's full text does not make the claim, on every count that matters: it does NOT state the
literature lacks defensible negative substrate determinations; it sets NO evidential bar; it does
NOT construct a substrate-vs-nonsubstrate design; it does NOT say such a grouping cannot be
populated. It is mainly a tabulation of LABELING OUTCOMES and follow-up actions, not an audit of
the evidence behind classifications.

And decisively, on the exact point §3.2 turns on: **availability of a substrate study and a
positive substrate result are distinct categories in its tables, and it does not characterise
every other drug as a tested negative.**

The abstract's framing *sounded* like §3.2's claim. The paper's actual object is different. **That
is the house defect, committed by me, in the section announcing the sweep for it** — a correct
reading of a real abstract attributed to the wrong SUBJECT, where the subject is what the paper
is actually about.

§3.2 now stands: unrefuted by this paper, still UNESTABLISHED (a probe cannot establish novelty).
Closest remaining threat: **Cicali 2020** (`10.1002/cpt.1768`), a scoping review of *the evidence
behind* CYP2D6 inhibitor classifications — which IS the evidence-audit move §3.2 claims.
**Unread: no PDF available.**

### §7.4's labeling lead is much weaker, and the paper cautions against my exact inference

The no-label evidence sits in FDA review documents and labels, and the paper does **not**
establish that any of it was peer-reviewed. The decisive distinction: those are **negative DDI
findings / rationales for not labeling an interaction** — NOT determinations that a compound **is
not a substrate**. The reading is explicit that they "should not be recast as a general,
evidentially standardized set of 'not a substrate' determinations". Plus the paper's own
provenance caveat: where FDA acceptance was not stated, the analysis reflected **the sponsor's
claim**, so these are not uniformly independent regulatory conclusions.

## THE NET EFFECT ON §1 IS TO STRENGTHEN IT

Opposite to how §7.4 read it. If the regulatory record holds no-DDI-label rationales rather than
standardized not-a-substrate determinations, then **the negative group is not quietly available
in labeling either**, and §1's finding is MORE robust than §7.4 implied.

Honest adjacent support, recorded verbatim with its difference stated: "Less information was
available for hepatic and renal transporter substrates compared to that for transporter
inhibition, potentially because drug developers do not necessitate the determination of whether
the drugs are substrates for all transporters". That is an asymmetry in **what gets determined at
all** — adjacent to §3.2, not the same claim.

**§1 is NOT amended; no scope qualifier written into §1.4** — §7.4 proposed one from an abstract
and §8 withdraws its ground. **§4 item 2 remains VERBATIM.**
