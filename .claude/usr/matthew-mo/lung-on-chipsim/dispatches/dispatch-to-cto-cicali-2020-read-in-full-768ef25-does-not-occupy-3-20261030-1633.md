## CICALI 2020 DOES NOT OCCUPY §3.2'S GROUND — five specific counts

- **No evidential bar.** It pre-specifies numerical AUC cutoffs for classifying inhibition
  STRENGTH (strong ≥5-fold, moderate ≥2–<5, weak ≥1.25–<2). That is a *classification rule*, not
  an evidence-quality standard — nothing about study quality, certainty or sufficiency.
- **No asymmetry analysis.** It does not investigate whether negatives are missing or rarely
  published. That is the core of §3.2 and the paper simply does not ask it.
- **No positive-vs-negative grouping**, and no claim that one cannot be assembled. Its contrast
  is *FDA-listed category vs observed AUC-derived category*.
- **No transporters at all** — CYP2D6 and metabolic enzymes only.
- Its actual claim is **different and narrower**: FDA-listed moderate/weak CYP2D6 inhibitor
  classifications do not always match classifications derived from observed AUC changes.

Cited from the full text: **55 of 89** pairs concordant (**62%**); **27 of 48** (**56%**) for
sensitive substrates; it recommends removing one FDA table entry and downgrading two.

## THE MORE USEFUL RESULT: PRECEDENT FOR THE METHOD, NOT PRIOR ART FOR THE CLAIM

Reading it turned up something better than a cleared threat. Cicali 2020 establishes that
**auditing the evidence behind a published classification is an established, publishable move
that finds real defects** — 38% of the audited classifications did not match the observed data.

§3.2 can cite it to show the **method** is legitimate and consequential, while remaining what
that paper is not: a statement about the **availability of negative determinations**, on
**transporters**, at a **stated evidential bar**.

**Precedent for a method strengthens a paper; prior art for a claim sinks it.** This is the first
and not the second — and that distinction is only visible because the full text was read rather
than inferred from the title, which §9.3 forbade and §8 explains why.
