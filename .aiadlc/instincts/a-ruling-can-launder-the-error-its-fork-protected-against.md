When a principal resolves a fork, the losing reading often encoded a real constraint — and adopting the winner can silently re-admit the exact error the fork was guarding. Case: STORM radiolabels a three-component formulation, so 'per-molecule deposition' was the strict reading. Ruling went to formulation-level (count stays 6). Had the caveat not been made part of the ruling, every later citation would have read as per-molecule target-site evidence — reintroducing the misstate-the-source defect the audit existed to catch. **Why:** a ruling feels like closure, so drafters stop asking what the rejected option was protecting. The caveat also decays fast if filed under 'limitations', where nobody reaching for a citation will meet it. **How to apply:** when recording a ruling, write the binding consequence BESIDE the ruling, not in a limitations section, and state it as part of the ruling rather than a note on it. Keep the rejected reading in the record marked rejected-not-deleted, so a later reader sees the fork and which way it went. And separate the ratification states explicitly: 'the COUNT is ruled; the per-row verdicts remain drafted' are two states, and implying one from the other is how an unratified row gets treated as settled.
